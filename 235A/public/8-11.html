8.11 The Cauchy distribution

We say that $X$ has the Cauchy distribution, and denote $X \sim \textrm{Cauchy}$, if $X$ has the density

$$ f_X(x) = \frac{1}{\pi} \frac{1}{1+x^2}. $$

$\textbf{Properties:}$

  1. $\mathbf{E} |X|=\infty$, i.e., the Cauchy distribution has no expectation.

  2. If $X,Y \sim \textrm{Cauchy}$ are independent then their average $\frac12(X+Y)$ is also distributed according to the Cauchy distribution.

  3. More generally, if $X_1,\ldots,X_n \sim \textrm{Cauchy}$ and $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\ge 0$ are numbers such that $\sum_j \alpha_j = 1$, then the weighted average

    $$ \sum_{j=1}^n \alpha_j X_j \sim \textrm{Cauchy}. $$

  4. If $\Theta \sim U[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ then $X=\tan \Theta \sim \textrm{Cauchy}$.
